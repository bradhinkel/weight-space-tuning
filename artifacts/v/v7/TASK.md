TASK. You are given the weights of an image classifier: a ResNet-50 (torchvision layout: conv1/bn1, layer1 to layer4 with 3, 4, 6 and 3 bottleneck blocks, global average pooling to 2,048 features, and a last layer fc of shape [2, 2048]; output 0 = landbird, 1 = waterbird), pretrained on ImageNet and then fine-tuned to tell waterbirds from landbirds on the Waterbirds dataset (bird photos pasted onto photos of land or water backgrounds). In the fine-tuning data, 95% of waterbirds were on water backgrounds and 95% of landbirds on land backgrounds, and the model learned that shortcut: it relies on the background as well as on the bird. It is very accurate on birds whose background matches their kind, but much worse on waterbirds on land and landbirds on water. Your job is to repair this by EDITING ITS WEIGHTS DIRECTLY: find out what goes wrong inside the network and repair it with targeted changes.

THIS SESSION: BUILD ON EVERYTHING DONE SO FAR. You are not the first to work on this model. The folder prior/ holds (1) the current best known repair, DFR (Deep Feature Reweighting), a published method that refits only the last layer, run with 5 random seeds, and (2) 18 earlier sessions in which Fable 5.2 repaired this same model by editing its weights under these rules: their submitted edits, final reports and working notes. Start with prior/README.md. Your job is to produce the best repaired model you can, aiming to do better than DFR on the evaluation. You may start from DFR, from any earlier edit, from a combination, or from something new; compare them, learn from what the earlier sessions found and tried, and decide on the evidence. Bear in mind that DFR's practice scores are in-sample (it was fitted on the practice images) and that most practice numbers in the earlier reports are in-sample for that run's own edit, so compare candidates on practice images they were not fitted or chosen on.

THE GOAL. The evaluation sorts images into four groups by (bird, background): landbird/land, landbird/water, waterbird/land, waterbird/water.
1. Raise the WORST-GROUP ACCURACY: the lowest of the four groups' accuracies. On practice data the fine-tuned model's worst group is waterbird/land at 72.9%, and landbird/water is 82.6%.
2. Keep the MEAN ACCURACY high. Mean accuracy here is the four group accuracies weighted by each group's share of the fine-tuning data: landbird/land 0.7295, landbird/water 0.0384, waterbird/land 0.0117, waterbird/water 0.2204. The fine-tuned model scores 97.7% on practice. An edit that raises the worst group by making the model much worse on the common groups is a poor repair.
The target, on the evaluation: worst-group accuracy of at least 88% with mean accuracy of at least 94%.

HOW IT WILL BE MEASURED. After you finish, your edited model is evaluated on the Waterbirds test split (5,794 images, different photos from the practice images but drawn the same way; its groups are 2,255 / 2,255 / 642 / 642 images) with a fixed protocol: each image is resized to 256x256, center-cropped to 224x224 and normalized with the ImageNet mean and standard deviation; the model is in eval mode (batch-norm uses its stored running statistics); the prediction is the larger of the two outputs. The architecture and the preprocessing are fixed: only weight values can change (same tensor names and shapes; batch-norm running means and variances count as weights and may be edited). You never see the test images.

WHAT COUNTS. We want edits that come from understanding the network: find out what goes wrong, identify the components responsible (specific channels, directions in a layer's activations, blocks, or weights of the last layer) and change them in a way you can justify with evidence. Out of scope, and refused by save_edit: any edit that only multiplies a whole tensor by a constant.
In this session, fitting is allowed: besides edits you reason out, you may obtain weight values by fitting them to the practice labels, in closed form or iteratively (for example a linear or logistic regression from saved activations to labels, with scikit-learn), and you may compute the last layer (fc) on saved avgpool features to score such fits in the kernel. Fits must use activations saved by forward passes; the network itself is still not trained or run in the kernel (rules a and b).

HOW TO WORK.
1. Diagnose first. Look at what the fine-tuned model gets wrong, and look at some of those images. Find out where in the network the background information enters the decision, and how the last layer combines bird and background evidence.
2. Localize causally. That a component differs between groups is not enough. Before relying on a component, show that changing it changes the behaviour you predicted, and try several strengths to see a dose-response.
3. Measure with enough power. An accuracy over n images has a standard error of up to about 50/sqrt(n) percentage points: about 4.3 points for the 133-image groups. Compare the edited model with the original on the SAME images with a paired pass ("both"). The practice images are all you have, and the evaluation uses different images: an edit tuned on one set of practice images can look better there than it is. Keep an edit only if its gain is clearly larger than its standard error, and if you choose an edit by comparing several candidates, confirm the winner on practice images you did not use to choose it.
4. Plan your forward passes (see the budget above).
5. Work efficiently: state the hypothesis of each code run in its first comment line; load things once into kernel variables; print compact summaries, never whole arrays; keep work/notes.md up to date (findings with numbers, edits tried and their measured effect, the current best edit; it is shown to you again if the conversation is restarted); keep copies of good edits (for example copy work/edited to work/best_1/).
